The day my own fix started lying to me

Two evaluations of one transcript. They disagreed completely, and the reassuring one was wrong.

0%KEYWORD DETECTOR
100%LLM JUDGE

An adversarial suite against a guarded model returned a 0% attack success rate. An LLM judge, reading the same transcript, scored it 100%.

The cause was upstream of what I was looking at. PII masking ran before detection, so the keyword detector was reading a transcript with the attacker's signal already redacted out of it. The detector was not broken. The safety feature was eating the evidence.

Detection moved ahead of masking and every prior run was re-baselined. Which of the earlier clean results were actually clean is unrecoverable; the count starts from the fix.

A metric that agrees with you is not evidence. Every eval I have built since carries a second, differently-shaped reader whose only job is to disagree.

Testing a platform against what its own documentation promises

Advisory work at Senso.ai. Outside developer, no access, no briefing — just the docs and a hostile reading of them.

73DEFECTS LOGGED
7 / 2CLOSED / CHANGED THE PRODUCT
11,217WORDS UNREACHABLE

I froze twelve developer questions with pass conditions written before any run, then executed them in two lanes with different handicaps. Setting the bar first is the method: once you have seen the output, you will negotiate with yourself about what counts as passing.

The finding that mattered was not a defect. The company had published 11,217 words of product definition in a place nothing reading the documentation could reach. The product was well explained and the explanation was unreachable.

Seventy-three defects filed. Seven closed, two changed the product. The rest are a standing argument about what the docs are for.
