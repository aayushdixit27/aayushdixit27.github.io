Two things I found by refusing to believe a number

The day my own fix started lying to me

Two evaluations of one transcript. They disagreed completely, and the reassuring one was wrong.

0%KEYWORD DETECTOR
100%LLM JUDGE

Running an adversarial suite against a guarded model, the attack success rate came back clean. Zero. The kind of number you screenshot.

An LLM judge, reading the same transcript, scored it at 100%. Every attack had landed.

The cause sat upstream of the thing I was looking at. PII masking ran before detection, so by the time the keyword detector saw the transcript, the attacker's signal had already been redacted out of it. The detector was not broken. It was reading a document with the evidence removed — and the redaction was a safety feature.

Detection moved ahead of masking and every prior run was re-baselined. What I cannot tell you is which of the earlier clean results were actually clean. That data is gone, and rebuilding it would have cost more than it was worth, so the line got drawn and the count started from the fix.

The lesson was not about pipeline ordering. It was that a metric agreeing with you is not evidence. Every eval I have built since has a second, differently-shaped reader whose only job is to disagree.

Testing a platform against what its own documentation promises

Advisory work at Senso.ai. Outside developer, no access, no briefing — just the docs and a hostile reading of them.

73DEFECTS LOGGED
7 / 2CLOSED / CHANGED THE PRODUCT
11,217WORDS UNREACHABLE

I froze twelve developer questions with pass conditions written before any run, then executed them in two lanes with different handicaps. Setting the bar first is the whole method: once you have seen the output, you will negotiate with yourself about what counts as passing.

The finding that mattered most was not a bug. The company had published 11,217 words of product definition in a place that nothing reading the documentation could actually reach. The product was well explained and the explanation was unreachable. Two of the defects I filed changed the product; seven closed; the rest are a standing argument about what the docs are for.
